crabshell Docs

Widgets

Each widget is a value you build with method calls and hand to a parent with child. Unless a table says otherwise, a method takes a value or a closure.

What every widget takes

Method What it does
style(style) The widget's Style.
visible(bool) Whether it shows. Hidden, it frees its space and takes no input, and keeps its bindings and state.
geometry(signal) Writes the widget's rectangle to a Signal<Rect> after each layout that moves it, in the window's logical pixels.
mapped_geometry(signal) Writes where the widget is drawn, after its own and its ancestors' transforms, in the window's logical pixels.
mask_region(region) Shows the widget only inside a Region, in its own logical pixels. Clicks still land on the whole widget. See mask regions.

geometry lets one widget follow another. This indicator slides to where a tab ended up. 

let tab_rect = cx.signal(Rect::new(0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0));
let x = cx.animate(move || tab_rect.get().x, Transition::new(200));
// On the tab:        .geometry(tab_rect)
// On the indicator:  .style(move || INDICATOR.translate(x.get(), 0.0))

Row and Column

Row lays its children out left to right and Column top to bottom. 

Row::new()
  .gap(8)
  .padding(4, 8)
  .align(Align::Center)
  .child(Text::new("left"))
  .child(Spacer::new())
  .child(Text::new("right"))
Method What it does
child(widget) Adds one child.
children_keyed(items, key, view) Adds a child per item of a changing list. See lists.
exit(duration), displaced(transition) Animate keyed children that leave or move. See motion.
gap(px) Space between children.
padding(vertical, horizontal) Space inside the edges. It wins over the style's padding.
align(align) Where children sit across the direction: Align::Start, Center, End or Stretch, the default.
justify(justify) Where they go along it: Justify::Start, the default, Center, End, SpaceBetween, SpaceAround or SpaceEvenly.
wrap(bool) Starts a new line for children that don't fit.
reverse(bool) Runs right to left, or bottom to top.

A row with a style and no children is this toolkit's plain box, for a dot, a track or a divider. 

Row::new().style(Style::new().background(ACCENT).width(18.0).height(18.0).radius(9.0))

Stack

Stack draws its children on top of each other, each stretched to the size of the largest. Later children are on top. A child whose style sets left, top, right or bottom is placed from those edges instead. 

Stack::new()
  .child(Image::new("/usr/share/backgrounds/hills.jpg"))
  .child(Text::new("on top").style(Style::new().align_self(Align::End)))

Grid

Grid places children row by row into equal columns. A child spans cells with Style::new().span(columns, rows). 

Grid::new().columns(7).gap(4).child(day(1)).child(day(2))
Method What it does
columns(n) How many columns. The default is 1.
gap(px), row_gap(px), column_gap(px) Space between cells.
padding(vertical, horizontal) Space inside the edges.
child, children_keyed, exit, displaced As on a row.

Spacer

Spacer::new() takes the leftover space in a row or column. Several share it equally, or by their styles' grow.

Text

Text::new("volume")
Text::new(move || format!("{}%", volume.get()))
Text::new(move || title.get()).elide(Elide::End)
Text::rich(move || parse_markup(&body.get())).on_link(open)
Method What it does
elide(Elide) Cuts text that doesn't fit with "…" at the Start, Middle or End. None by default.
ellipsis(bool) Short for elide(Elide::End), or Elide::None when false.
max_lines(n) Lets the text wrap onto this many lines. The default is 1.
selectable(bool) Lets the pointer select the text, and Ctrl+C copy it. Inside a button, it takes the press, so the button doesn't click.
on_link(handler) Runs with &Cx and the URL when a link span is clicked, or opened with Enter or Space after Tab reached it.
link_hover_color(color) Draws a link under the pointer or the focus underlined, in this color. With None, the default, it keeps its own.

Text is black sans-serif at 13 pixels until a style says otherwise, and takes color, font, text_align, line_height, underline and strikethrough from the nearest widget above that sets each.

Text::rich(spans) shows a Vec<Span>. Span::new(text) takes bold(), italic(), underline(), strikethrough(), color(c) and link(url), and takes the rest from the text. parse_markup(body) reads a notification body's markup (b, i, u, a and img) into spans. It keeps only http, https and mailto links, shows an image's alt text and never loads it.

cx.measure_text(text, font) gives a TextMetrics with the width, height, ascent, descent and line height a Text in that font would have.

Its ink is the rectangle the glyphs actually cover. Icon fonts often draw wider than a glyph advances, so size an icon's box by ink.width and shift the text left by ink.x. 

let ink = cx.measure_text("\u{f028}", ICON_FONT).ink;
Text::new("\u{f028}").style(ICON.width(ink.width.ceil()).translate(-ink.x, 0.0))

Button

Button::new(child) is a clickable box that centers one child. It is also how any widget hears the pointer, so wrap a card in one to make it clickable. 

Button::new(Text::new("mute"))
  .style(CHIP)
  .hover_style(CHIP_HOVER)
  .on_click(|cx| cx.audio().toggle_mute())
Method What it does
hover_style(style) Laid over the style while the pointer is over the button.
pressed_style(style) Laid over both while it is held.
focus_style(style) Laid over the style while it has the keyboard focus.
enabled(bool) Off, it runs no handler, shows no state style and lets the pointer through.
cursor(shape) The pointer's shape over it. The default is the pointing hand.
focus(bool) Takes the keyboard focus whenever it turns true.
hovered(signal) Writes to a Signal<bool> whether the pointer is over it or anything inside.
pointer_position(signal) Writes the pointer's place to a Signal<Option<Point>>.
pressed(signal) Writes to a Signal<bool> whether the button is held, even after the pointer slides off it.

pressed lets you scale a glyph inside a button while it is held, without scaling the button's box. 

let held = cx.signal(false);
Button::new(Text::new("\u{f04b}").style(move || GLYPH.scale(if held.get() { 0.85 } else { 1.0 })))
  .pressed(held)
  .on_click(|cx| play_pause(cx))

Its handlers are on Pointer and keyboard.

Icon

Icon::new(source) draws an icon as a square. The source is a name from the icon theme, a file path, or an IconSource. 

Icon::new("audio-volume-high").size(16)
Icon::new("audio-volume-high-symbolic").tint(TEXT)
Icon::new(move || if muted.get() { "audio-volume-muted" } else { "audio-volume-high" })
Method What it does
size(px) The width and height. The default is 16.
tint(color) Paints every pixel in that color and keeps its alpha, for symbolic icons.

Names are looked up in GTK's icon theme, or hicolor without one, unless you call cx.set_icon_theme. See icons.

Image

Image::new(path) shows a PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP or SVG file in a box. 

Image::new("/home/me/Pictures/hills.jpg").width(320).height(180).fit(Fit::Cover)
Method What it does
width(px), height(px) The box the picture is drawn in.
fit(fit) Fit::Fill, the default, Contain or Cover.
tint(color) As on an icon.
brightness(n), contrast(n), saturation(n) Filters, where 1 leaves the picture as it is.
blur(px) A Gaussian blur.
playing(bool) Whether an animated GIF plays.
transition(transition) How a new source replaces the old one.
status(signal) Writes an ImageStatus to a signal: Empty, Loading, Ready or Failed.

status says whether the picture has loaded: Loading after each new source, then Ready or Failed once the picture is loaded, whether or not it shows. Failed means the file can't be read or is too large. Fade album art in on Ready with its opacity, or show a glyph on Failed. 

let status = cx.signal(ImageStatus::Empty);
let shown = cx.animate(
  move || if status.get() == ImageStatus::Ready { 1.0 } else { 0.0 },
  Transition::new(300),
);
Image::new(move || art.get())
  .width(96)
  .height(96)
  .status(status)
  .style(move || Style::new().opacity(shown.get()))

See images.

TextInput

TextInput::new(text) is a one-line field. It takes the Signal<String> it edits, so typing writes the signal and setting the signal changes the field. 

let query = cx.signal(String::new());
TextInput::new(query)
  .placeholder("search")
  .focus(true)
  .on_accept(move |_cx, text| println!("run {text}"))
  .style(FIELD)
Method What it does
placeholder(text) Shown dimmed while the field is empty.
password(bool) Shows a dot for each character.
focus(bool) Takes the keyboard focus whenever it turns true.
on_accept(handler) Runs with &Cx and the text when Enter is pressed.
max_length(n) At most n characters. Typing and pastes stop there, and text set from outside is cut and written back to the signal.
read_only(bool) Takes no edits while true. The text can still be selected and copied, unless it is a password.
selection_color(color) The selection's color. None, the default, is the text's color at a third of its alpha.
selected_text_color(color) The selected text's color. None, the default, keeps the text's own.

The field has a caret, a selection, word movement with Ctrl, the clipboard on Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X and Ctrl+V, and input methods. A password field pastes but never copies. Keys it doesn't use go on to the window's on_key. Its window needs a keyboard focus. Dragging selects, a double click selects a word, a triple click the line, and Shift with a click extends the selection. A drag after a double or triple click goes by whole words or lines.

A field takes the alignment, line height and underline its style sets or inherits, as a Text does.

TextArea

TextArea::new(text) is a multi-line editor. It takes the Signal<String> it edits, as a field does, and takes placeholder, focus, wrap, on_accept, max_length, read_only, selection_color and selected_text_color. Its max_length counts a line break as one character. 

let note = cx.persist("note", String::new());
ScrollView::new(TextArea::new(note).placeholder("jot something down"))
  .style(NOTE)

Enter breaks the line and Ctrl+Enter runs on_accept. Up and Down move by line, Home and End go to the ends of the wrapped line, and PageUp and PageDown move by the height of the scroll view around it. It wraps at words to its width, 200 pixels by default; wrap(false) lets lines run on. Inside a ScrollView, the view scrolls to keep the caret shown. Tab types a tab, four spaces wide, as Qt's TextEdit does, and Shift+Tab and Ctrl+Tab move the focus.

Slider

Slider::new(value) is a handle on a track. It takes the Signal<f32> it writes. 

let volume = cx.signal(50.0_f32);
Slider::new(volume)
  .range(0.0, 100.0)
  .step(1.0)
  .style(TRACK)
  .fill_style(FILL)
  .handle_style(HANDLE)
Method What it does
range(min, max) The ends. The default is 0 to 1.
step(n) What values snap to. 0, the default, doesn't snap.
limit(max) The highest value a drag, the wheel or a key can set. The track still shows the whole range.
wheel_step(n) How far a wheel notch or an arrow key moves it. The default is the step, or a twentieth of the range without one.
vertical(bool) Runs it bottom to top.
enabled(bool) As on a button.
style(style) The track, 120 by 4 pixels unless the style sets a size.
fill_style(style) The part from the start to the handle.
handle_style(style) The handle, 16 by 16 unless the style sets a size.
dragging(signal) Writes to a Signal<bool> whether the handle is held.

A press, a drag, the wheel and the arrow keys all move it.

limit keeps a slider under a cap without shrinking its track. A brightness slider held to half in a power saver mode still shows 0 to 100, and nothing moves it past 50. wheel_step lets drags give whole values while the wheel moves 5. 

Slider::new(brightness)
  .range(0.0, 100.0)
  .step(1.0)
  .wheel_step(5.0)
  .limit(move || if saver.get() { Some(50.0) } else { None })

To bind a slider to an outside value such as a sink's volume, copy the outside value in only while dragging is false, and send the slider's value out only when it differs. The control example does this.

ScrollView

ScrollView::new(child) shows part of a child larger than its box. The wheel, the touchpad, a drag and its scroll bar move it. 

ScrollView::new(Column::new().children_keyed(items, key, row))
  .style(Style::new().height(300.0))
  .scroll_bar(Style::new().background(RAISED).radius(3.0))
Method What it does
offset(signal) A Signal<f32> holding how far it is scrolled, written both ways.
horizontal(bool) Scrolls sideways instead.
both(bool) Scrolls down and across, each axis with its own fling and spring. offset is then the vertical offset.
offset_x(signal) The horizontal offset of a view that scrolls both ways.
kinetic(bool) Keeps moving after a fling. On by default.
overshoot(bool) Lets a drag pull past an end and spring back. On by default.
mouse_drag(bool) Lets the mouse drag the content as a finger does. On by default; off, a mouse press that moves still clicks.
scroll_bar(style) Draws a handle from the style's background, radius and width, one an axis. None by default. A press on the track pages toward it, and held, keeps paging every 50 ms.

ListView

ListView::new(items, key, view) is a scrolling list that builds only the rows in view. It takes the same three closures as children_keyed. 

ListView::new(
  move || shown.get(),
  |app| app.clone(),
  move |_cx, app| Row::new().style(ROW).child(Text::new(move || app.get())),
)
.row_height(28)
.height(280)
.current(move || Some(picked.get()))
.highlight(PICKED)
Method What it does
row_height(px) The least height of a row. A row grows to fit its content.
height(px) The height of the list's box.
current(index) An Option<usize> naming the row to keep in view.
highlight(style) A box behind the current row, which slides and resizes over the style's transition.
horizontal(bool) Runs the rows left to right.
offset, kinetic, overshoot, mouse_drag, scroll_bar As on a scroll view.

GridView

GridView::new(items, key, view) lays cells of one size out in as many columns as fit and builds only the rows in view, for an application grid or a wallpaper picker. 

GridView::new(move || wallpapers.get(), |path| path.clone(), thumbnail)
  .cell_size(160, 100)
  .height(420)

It takes cell_size(width, height) and, as a list view does, height, current, highlight, offset, kinetic, overshoot, mouse_drag and scroll_bar.

horizontal(true) fills each column top to bottom, then the next, and scrolls sideways; height then means the grid's width. Cells stay one size; a wrapping Row holds cells of different sizes.
